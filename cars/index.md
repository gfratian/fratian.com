People measure their lives in all sorts of units. Jobs, houses, haircuts. I seem
to measure mine in cars — so here they are, roughly in the order they turned up,
each one a little time capsule of whoever I was when I bought it.

(One of them, the Audi S8 called Rocinante, got so out of hand it earned its own
book. That story lives in [Roci and I](/travel/).)

## VW Golf TD — *the first one* (~1987, dark blue)

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/golf.jpg">📷 photo to come — VW Golf TD (a representative example, not my actual car)</div>

Everyone should learn on something this honest. Mine was dark blue, with those
funny little triangular vent windows up front that you could crack open with a
thumb, and a diesel engine that clattered like a bag of spanners and would
apparently run forever out of spite. Slow? Gloriously. But it went, and it was
mine, and nothing since has felt quite that big a deal.

## Honda Prelude — *the coupe years* (1992, black)

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/prelude.jpg">📷 photo to come — Honda Prelude</div>

Low, wide, black, and far more serious about corners than a car that shape had
any right to be. The Prelude was where I learned that a car could be a hobby and
not just an appliance.

## Mitsubishi Montero SR — *the go-anywhere phase* (1996)

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/montero.jpg">📷 photo to come — Mitsubishi Montero SR</div>

Big, boxy, V6, and utterly unbothered by weather, terrain, or good sense. If the
Prelude was about corners, the Montero was about not caring whether there was a
road there at all.

## Honda Odyssey — *the sensible chapter* (2001)

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/odyssey.jpg">📷 photo to come — Honda Odyssey</div>

The least glamorous and most useful vehicle I have ever owned. Every life needs
a minivan era, and I am not too proud to admit I enjoyed mine.

## Alfa Romeo 4C — *the head-over-heart one*

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/alfa-4c.jpg">📷 photo to come — Alfa Romeo 4C</div>

A carbon-fibre tub, an engine behind your shoulders, no power steering, and no
interest whatsoever in your comfort. The 4C doesn't do sensible. It does
*Italian*, which is a different and much louder thing.

## BMW 3.0 CS — *the beautiful one*

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/bmw-30cs.jpg">📷 photo to come — BMW 3.0 CS</div>

The oldest and easily the prettiest thing in the garage — a 1970s coupe with
chrome, glass, and proportions they simply don't draw any more. You don't so much
drive it as apologise to it.

## BMW 535d — *the quietly rapid one*

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/bmw-535d.jpg">📷 photo to come — BMW 535d</div>

A diesel that has no business being this quick. All torque, all manners, the kind
of car that covers a stupid distance before you've noticed you left.

## BMW 640i — *the grand tourer*

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/bmw-640i.jpg">📷 photo to come — BMW 640i</div>

A silky straight-six and a long nose pointed at the horizon. Not the fastest thing
here, but possibly the one I'd pick for a day I didn't want to end.

## BMW 840i — *the big coupe*

<div class="car-photo" data-src="/assets/cars/bmw-840i.jpg">📷 photo to come — BMW 840i</div>

The current chapter, and a fittingly large one — a proper two-door GT that makes
every drive feel like it's going somewhere worth arriving.
